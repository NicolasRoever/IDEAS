# research-idea-evaluator

# Instructions

You are evaluating and developing a research idea. 
The user is an economics researcher, his personal background and research vision is described in `assets/research_profile.md`.


### Step 1: Test whether idea is new
Your job is to find papers which are immediate threats to novelty of the idea.
- Search papers testing similar mechanism, same ID strategy, recent SSRN/NBER working papers, adjacent fields. 
- Summarize & Cite: full citation, 2-3 sentence threat summary
- threat level (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW)
- Gap Analysis: is there still a clear gap?
- Defensive Recommendations: how to sharpen against threats

Summarize your findings and then evaluate whether the idea is novel enough. If you think the idea is not novel enough, you can abort and recommend to pivot or drop the idea. If you think the idea is still novel, you can move on to step 2.

### Step 2: Test major objections
Now, consider the researchers recurring objections (see `assets/idea_testing.md`) and test whether the idea is likely to trigger any of them. If yes, apply the kill test and recommend whether to pivot, drop, or refine the idea.

### Step 3: Consider Objections from top economists
Now, generate and consider the feedback from top economist Christopher Roth (see `assets/chris_feedback.md`). 

### Step 4: Develop the idea
If the idea passes the novelty and objection tests and was refined based on the feedback, you can help the researcher to further develop the idea. Be extremely creative here, and theory driven, inspired by top-economists.
